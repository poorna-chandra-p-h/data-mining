Section A, Part (c): Sublinear Retrieval & Risk PricingTo achieve sublinear retrieval and avoid an $O(N^2)$ full-corpus comparison, I implemented a Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) candidate retrieval structure over the 400-integer MinHash signatures.LSH exposes a tunable tension based on how the signature is divided into $b$ bands and $r$ rows ($b \times r = k$). A pair only survives to the candidate stage if they match perfectly on at least one band. The probability of survival as a function of true similarity $s$ is governed by the S-curve: $P = 1 - (1 - s^r)^b$. The approximate similarity threshold is $(1/b)^{1/r}$.Pricing the Risk (Business Justification):The Head of Product explicitly stated that the two failure modes have vastly asymmetrical costs.Cost of a False Positive (False Merge): Catastrophic. A bidder misses a deadline and sues the company.Cost of a False Negative (Failure to Merge): Low. A bidder sees a duplicate card and grumbles.Because the cost of a false positive is intolerably high, I must deliberately sacrifice recall (accepting more false negatives) to protect precision. Therefore, I pushed the similarity threshold very high to ensure only near-identical notices merge.I configured LSH with $b = 20$ bands and $r = 20$ rows.As demonstrated in the generated plot and calculated by the script, this configuration yields an operating threshold of 0.861 ($(1/20)^{1/20}$). This strict setting provides massive mathematical protection against false positives, directly reflecting the Head of Product's risk asymmetry.
